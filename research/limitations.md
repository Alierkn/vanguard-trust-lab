# Limitations

1. **No live product measurement.** The public project intentionally contains no startup, performance, process, driver, network, telemetry, or compatibility measurement.
2. **Proprietary implementation.** Vanguard source code, detection logic, backend policy, and complete telemetry are unavailable.
3. **First-party evidence.** Riot architecture, privacy, and effectiveness descriptions are vendor statements rather than independent experimental results.
4. **Platform/product gap.** Microsoft documentation establishes Windows mechanisms but cannot prove Riot's exact use, validation policy, or backend interpretation.
5. **Simplified model.** The architecture omits implementation detail and is not a verified data-flow diagram.
6. **Educational Lab.** Manual switches and local hashing do not reproduce Vanguard Pre-Check, attestation, anti-cheat detection, or a host-security verdict.
7. **Temporal validity.** Public pages and product requirements may change after the 12 July 2026 evidence snapshot.
8. **Generalization.** Conclusions should not be transferred automatically to every anti-cheat, endpoint product, operating system, or hardware platform.

These limitations are part of the result. The project can explain a documented trust model and its trade-offs; it cannot certify a product or a device.
